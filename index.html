Market Recommendation (Quarter Kelly)

Enter bookmaker odds above, then predict. The system identifies which market offers the best value using the Kelly Criterion.

How it works: The Kelly Criterion calculates optimal stake sizing based on your edge. Edge = Model Probability - Bookmaker Implied Probability. If your model says 72% but the bookmaker implies 65% (odds 1.54), you have a +7% edge. Full Kelly = (bp - q) / b where b = odds-1, p = your prob, q = 1-p. Quarter Kelly (25% of full Kelly) is used for bankroll management — it dramatically reduces variance while still capturing most of the long-term growth. A market needs positive edge to warrant any bet.

Z-Score Analysis

Statistical significance of recent performance deviation from the mean

How it works: Z-Score measures how many standard deviations recent form is from the seasonal mean. Unlike simple percentage deviation, it accounts for team consistency. A team with high variance may frequently deviate by 20%, but a Z-score of -2.0 means that deviation is statistically significant regardless of variance. Thresholds: < -1.5 = Strong Underperformance (regression UP likely), -1.5 to -1.0 = Moderate Under, -1.0 to +1.0 = Normal, +1.0 to +1.5 = Moderate Over, > +1.5 = Strong Overperformance (regression DOWN likely).

Backtest Mode: Pattern Analysis

Retroactively run the prediction engine on all loaded matches to find winning & losing patterns

How it works: For each match, the engine builds a "leave-one-out" model using all OTHER matches as training data (no look-ahead bias). It then records what the model predicted vs what actually happened, identifying which signals, confidence levels, and probability ranges correlate with actual results. The Sweet Spot criteria are also retroactively evaluated to show their historical hit rate.

Data-Driven Sweet Spot Discovery

How it works: The engine tests hundreds of possible criteria ranges and combinations against your actual match results. For each numerical feature (O2.5 prob, O1.5 odds, BTTS odds, clean sheet %), it tries different boundary ranges. For categorical features (regression signal, Z-Score signal, BTTS confidence), it tests each category. Then it searches for the best 2-criterion and 3-criterion combinations that produce the highest O2.5 hit rate with at least 3 matches. The result: data-proven sweet spot criteria derived from your specific dataset.
